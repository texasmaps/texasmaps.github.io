Four ways to read the same votes (through 2026-10-19). Pick a tab to recolour the map; every county hover shows all four. Thirteen public polls (Sept 18 to Oct 19) put Hinojosa at 48.8%. Since then, many people who do not usually vote have voted early, and the lists show they lean her way. The question is whether the polls already counted them.
Headline: we do not know how many of them the polls counted, so each of the 10,000 runs assumes a different share, from none to all. Result: Hinojosa +1.0, Hinojosa wins 56% of runs.
If the polls caught the surge: the 48.8% already includes the new voters. Result: Abbott +2.2, Hinojosa wins 34% of runs.
If the polls missed the surge: the new voters count on top of the 48.8%. Result: Hinojosa +4.1, Hinojosa wins 75% of runs.
If 2022 repeats: everyone votes as their neighbourhood did in 2022, no polls at all. Result: Abbott +3.8, Hinojosa wins 11% of runs.
If the polls are as wrong as they were in 2024, Abbott by about 10. Election night tells us which reading was right: about 52% of the early vote for Hinojosa points to the polls having caught the surge, about 54% to their having missed it.
