# How to Read the Daily Maps

There are two maps. **Texas Early Vote Snapshot** shows where every county stands today. **Texas Race Tracker** shows how they're moving.

## Start with the status line
Every map's title shows how much data it rests on, for example "Through Oct 21: 241/254 counties complete · 99% of votes on the list · 21% of expected vote in."
- **Counties complete and votes on the list:** whether the state's files have caught up. Low numbers mean the map may shift as files are revised.
- **Expected vote in:** how much of the race has actually been cast. Early on, projections lean mostly on polls and history.

## Colors and markings, on every view
- **Blue** = Hinojosa ahead. **Red** = Abbott ahead.
- **Deeper color** = more confident or a bigger margin. **Paler** = less certain.
- **Hatched** = not rated, because the county's voter list is too incomplete.
- **Tan** = not called, because primary history says little about November votes there.
- **Grey** = fewer than 500 votes.
- **Lighter shading** = projected mostly from history; little of this year's vote is in.
- **Outline** = on the watch list of close counties.
- **Small symbol** = the leader flips depending on whether the model starts from 2022 or 2024.

## Ratings (same scale everywhere)
| Rating | Chance the leader wins |
|---|---|
| Safe | 95%+ |
| Likely | 80–95% |
| Lean | 65–80% |
| Tilt | 55–65% |
| Toss-up | under 55%, still showing which way it tips |

## Texas Early Vote Snapshot: seven views

1. **Close Counties** (default)
   - **Color:** who leads, deeper for more confidence.
   - **Labels:** outlined counties carry their projected margin and odds, for example "Hinojosa +2.4 · 62%."
   - **Use it to:** see which counties could decide a tight race and which way each is tipping.
2. **Final Projection:** each county's projected final margin, out of all votes. This is the bottom line for each county.
3. **Early Vote Lean:** the estimated split among people who have already voted. Early voters often lean more Democratic than Election Day voters, so a blue early vote can still end red.
4. **Early Vote Lead Odds:** the chance Hinojosa leads each county's early vote. 50% is even, toward 100% she likely leads, toward 0% Abbott likely does.
5. **Who's Voting:** the early-voter mix by primary history. Real data, no modeling. It shows which party's habitual voters are showing up.
6. **Pace vs. 2022:** turnout against the same day of early voting in 2022. Green is ahead, purple behind. Real data. A county can be ahead just because more people vote early now, so check Who's Voting to see who's driving it.
7. **2022 Benchmark:** the actual 2022 early-vote result. Compare it with Early Vote Lean to see where counties have moved.

## Texas Race Tracker
- **County color:** the latest projected final margin, with every day so far combined.
- **Arrows:** the change since yesterday, toward Hinojosa or toward Abbott. Arrows appear only where the data was complete on both days, so late file revisions don't look like movement.
- **Hover:** the change since day 1, plus the county's full details.
- **Day slider:** step through the days to see how each county's projection built up.
- **Side chart:** the statewide projected margin and win chance over time.

**Read it for trends:** several days moving the same way means more than one big jump. One-day jumps often come from files catching up.

## Hover, on any county
- Projected result and range
- Win chance and rating
- Chance of finishing within 3 points
- Early voters so far
- Completeness and share of expected vote in
- What changed since yesterday, and why: new votes, late revisions, a correction, or a new poll

## Quick reading order
1. **Check the status line.** How solid is today's data?
2. **Open Close Counties.** Which counties are close, and which way do they tip?
3. **Check the Race Tracker arrows.** Which way are those counties moving?
4. **Use Who's Voting and Pace vs. 2022** to see what's driving the movement.

Every map is a model estimate from public data, not a vote count.
