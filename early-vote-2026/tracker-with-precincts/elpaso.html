Democratic base still out (the chase universe)Who: people who voted in the Democratic primary in March 2022, 2024 or 2026 and never in a Republican one, counted in the precinct of their most recent record, who have not yet shown up on any 2026 early-voting list in Texas. Why it matters: these are the likeliest Democratic votes still out there. Two parts: people who voted in the March 2026 primary (current addresses, most reliable) and people seen only in 2022 to 2024 (older records; some have moved).
No-party regular voters still outWho: people who have never taken part in a party primary but did vote in the 2022 or 2024 general election, and have not voted yet. Why it matters: they do turn out. The data cannot say how they vote, so look at the precinct's 2022 and 2024 results, shown beside every count, to judge which way they lean.
Registered, no recent voteWho: people on the November 2024 rolls with no vote in the 2022 or 2024 general election, no primary record (March 2022, 2024 or 2026), and no early vote this year. The November 2025 amendment election is not counted as a vote. Approximate: there is no voter file, so this is registration minus known voters; it includes inactive registrations and people who have moved. Why it matters: the biggest pool and the hardest to move.
Total potentialWhat: the three groups added together. They do not overlap, so the total is everyone in the precinct who could still vote. How to use it: start with the Democratic base, then the regulars in precincts that lean Democratic, then the third group where there is capacity.

Good to know. Registered voters and the 2024 turnout per precinct are the state's November 2024 figures, the latest it publishes by precinct. Precincts created or renumbered since then have no boundary on the map yet (Fort Bend and Tarrant renumbered many); they stay in the table and the rankings, marked "no boundary". The state's 2022 and 2024 Election Day rosters carry no county, so the 2022 to 2024-only part of the Democratic base and the regular-voter group are undercounts; the 2026-primary part is complete. Primary records are the March primaries of 2022, 2024 and 2026 (2020 is not available; the May runoffs are not used). Mail voters count in their precinct. The chase universe leaves out anyone who has already voted in another county.

About the data

Early voters: Texas Secretary of State daily lists (every version combined), plus Fort Bend's own check-in list. Party record: March 2022, 2024 and 2026 primary rosters. Past voting and location: Nov. 2022 and Nov. 2024 general rosters (the Nov. 2025 amendment roster only locates voters). Precinct shapes, results, registration: Texas Legislative Council (2024 map, 2022 governor, 2024 president, Nov. 2024 registration), checked against the official canvass. Polling places: the Secretary of State's polling-place file for this election (sites, addresses, hours by day), placed with the U.S. Census Bureau geocoder; Travis sites checked against the county's own lists. Address search: U.S. Census Bureau geocoder. Base map: Esri, OpenStreetMap.
No voter file, names or IDs; no predictions. Registration is as of Nov. 2024, so turnout shares are approximate.
